The conditions for performance

What sales enablement is for, and how to lead it so it reaches the field

Enablement changes the conditions under which performance happens. Content is one condition among several, and rarely the binding one. Three chapters test that against real programmes. The last asks what it implies next.

Learning objectives

01

Prove why attendance, completion and satisfaction fail as measures, and what to use instead.

02

Diagnose whether a performance problem is a training problem, using a model rather than instinct.

03

Design backwards from the performance you want, and name who owns the coaching.

04

State what your evidence establishes and what it does not, including when the evidence is yours.

05

Hold an unproven idea to the same standard by specifying its failure condition before building.

Enablement mission

Meet Maya

TheoryKirkpatrick Level 1 measures whether people enjoyed the session. It does not predict behaviour, and it is routinely presented as a quality signal.

The reframe

Start from what sellers need to know and you will build a resource library. Maya changes the question. What has to be true for a seller to hold a conversation that moves revenue. The answer runs to six items. Content is one of them and it is not near the top.

Learning point

The framing question decides the output, and it is settled before any design work starts. A leader who cannot name the binding constraint will build the wrong solution.

TheoryGilbert's Behaviour Engineering Model splits environment (information, instruments, incentives) from individual (knowledge, capacity, motives), and finds the environment explains more variance. Mager and Pipe give the blunt version: if you held a gun to their head, could they do it? If yes, training will not fix it.

Inspect what you expect

Maya writes two columns. Everything she can measure today, and everything she would need to measure to know whether any of it worked. The first column is already wired up. The second needs analytics to agree a method, access to call data, and sales leaders willing to be measured alongside her. She takes the second.

Learning point

Moving to performance measurement is a negotiation before it is an analysis. Expect the conversation with analytics and sales leadership to be harder than the maths. The right-hand column is still not causal. Pipeline progression moves with pricing, territory and competitors. Better than completions, not the same as attribution.

Chapter 2

Begin with the end in mind

Hundreds of sellers across several regions, thousands of recorded calls. Qualification is inconsistent, discovery is shallow, too many conversations lead with product. Enablement is being judged on attendance and completion, so none of this shows up anywhere.

The diagnosis

Maya goes looking for a skill gap and does not find one. The sellers have the information. What she finds is that where frontline leaders do not coach a method, sellers treat the training as an interruption to the real job. The method dies somewhere between the classroom and the pipeline review.

Learning point

Capability has to be built as a system. Leaders own the coaching, and proficiency is demonstrated before anyone works unassisted.

TheoryVygotsky's zone of proximal development is the gap between what someone can do alone and what they can do with guidance. Content closes the first. Only a coach closes the second.

Designing backwards

Maya works backwards from the conversation she wants. Short self-paced pre-work instead of the multi-hour lecture. Mandatory roleplay on real objections instead of theoretical discussion. And no live territory until a leader has graded the assessment.

Learning point

The third move is the one that matters. Gating territory behind demonstrated proficiency turns training from an event into a condition of doing the job. It also delays revenue activity, which is why it needs a sponsor.

TheoryRoediger and Karpicke found that being tested beats restudying for retention, by a wide margin and with strong replication. The roleplay is a learning event, not a measurement event.

Leaders are multipliers

Frontline leaders have to be empowered and enabled to drive the behaviour change. This is the make or break moment. Two things turn them into owners. A scorecard, so reviewing a call is structured rather than impressionistic. And a data feed into meetings they already hold, so the review has somewhere to live.

If you want a behaviour from frontline leaders, put it inside a meeting that already exists. A new meeting is a tax. An extra item is a habit.

The outcome

Leaders moved from observers to coaching owners, with call reviews sitting inside the weekly cadence. Sellers who scored higher showed stronger pipeline progression, deal velocity and deal size. That last sentence is an association. Maya says so every time she presents it.

Learning point

The association is real and worth having. It is also what you would see if good sellers happen to score well and sell well for unrelated reasons. Saying so costs nothing and buys credibility you will need later.

EvidenceEstablishes: a repeatable way to test whether enablement reaches field behaviour and deals. Does not establish: that the programme caused the commercial outcomes. Nobody was randomly assigned.

Capability, not calendar, drives the ramp

Learning point

An onboarding programme that is mostly content is answering a different question, and will produce hires who have completed a great deal and can do very little.

TheorySelf-Determination Theory, Deci and Ryan: durable motivation needs autonomy, competence and relatedness. A new hire with content and no relatedness has one of three.

Three milestones

Three milestones, no fixed duration. Role and tooling first. Then product demonstration and objection handling. Then territory planning and live conversations. A hire moves when they demonstrate capability. Each region gets its own narrative content, built on the engine in chapter four, so local relevance does not cost six weeks.

Learning point

Gating on capability rather than time is the same move as gating territory in chapter two, and it is the most transferable decision in this course.

TheoryMihaly Csikszentmihalyi named flow, the highly focused mental state where challenge and skill are matched. His work on challenge-skill balance holds that a task far above current skill produces anxiety, far below produces boredom, and neither produces learning. A fixed calendar guarantees both, to different people.

Learning point

Vague ownership is a design problem, not a documentation one. A responsibility with no name against it belongs to whoever has least to do, which is usually nobody.

TheoryEdmondson on psychological safety explains why the buddy has to be a peer. People withhold questions and admissions of not knowing from whoever evaluates them.

The outcome

Against historical cohorts the pilot showed faster role readiness and stronger customer empathy. Ramp improved by fifty per cent. Leader coordination dropped to under thirty minutes a week. The pilot was three people, far too small to call any of it proof. It is a good signal and Maya presents it as one, asterisk attached.

Chapter 4

Scale without losing fidelity

The bottleneck

Every market needs localised enablement. Manual authoring takes six to eight weeks per course. So regions do the rational thing and run the generic global version, which lands badly, which confirms their suspicion that central enablement does not understand their market. Maya times the process. The bottleneck is manual asset handling and slow review, not instructional design. She looks at off-the-shelf generative tools as a shortcut and finds they invent product interfaces that do not exist, which rules them out for anything teaching a real product.

Learning point

Time the process before optimising it. The constraint is rarely where the expertise sits. And a tool that is confidently wrong about your product is worse than no tool, because the error arrives looking finished.

Structure and evidence

AI drafts region-specific storyboards from approved product and value-proposition sources, then generates the module code and the packaging. Subject matter experts supply real captured screen recordings for every workflow. A shared wireframe and review standard lets any designer on a global team produce consistent modules.

Learning point

Let the model do structure, sequencing, drafting and packaging. Keep it away from the evidence, which is what it fabricates most convincingly. The review standard is what makes output consistent across a distributed team, not the model.

The outcome

Production time fell from six weeks to under four days per market, and the four days includes recording, review and sign-off rather than excluding them. Three regional learning paths ran in parallel. This is a speed number. Maya never lets a steering meeting hear it as an effectiveness number.

Learning point

Production efficiency is a legitimate result and does not need to borrow authority from a learning claim. The temptation to let the two blur is strong, because nobody in the room will correct you.

EvidenceEstablishes: a large, verifiable cut in cycle time, including the review stages usually left out of such claims. Does not establish: that the faster modules taught anyone anything.

Two failures. Early AI-generated interface mockups were wrong, so Maya now uses captured recordings for product workflows with no exceptions. And regional variants with no named owner became a patching burden the first time a feature changed, because updating them was nobody's job.

Learning point

The second failure is an ownership problem: a shared resource with no named owner degrades. Maya now agrees content ownership and update commitments with local teams before a multi-market build starts. Scaling production without scaling maintenance moves the bottleneck downstream.

Chapter 5

The simulator

An unbuilt idea, held to the same standard
The tennis question

Maya keeps turning over a question. To get better at tennis she would not take an online course on tennis and she would not go to the gym. She would play tennis, with somebody watching who could tell her what to change. Three programmes in, everything she has built still sits at some distance from that.

What practice requires

There is a popular version of this that says ten thousand hours of practice produces expertise. The number comes from Gladwell's reading of one study of violinists, where ten thousand was an average accumulated by a certain age rather than a threshold. Ericsson, whose research it was, spent years objecting to the framing. What his work specifies instead is four conditions, and they are far more useful than a number.

A defined task at the edge of current ability
Immediate informative feedback
Repetition with error correction
A coach who designs the task
10,000 hours — Gladwell's reading of one study of violinists. An average, not a threshold.
Learning point

Maya uses the four conditions as a design specification. Any proposed practice environment can be checked against them. A number cannot be checked against anything.

TheoryEricsson, with the boundary stated: the evidence is strongest in domains with stable rules, objective measures and established training methods. Chess, music and sport qualify. Sales does not, fully. The rules shift, feedback is delayed and noisy, and what counts as a good conversation is contested. The approach still holds. The claim has to be narrower.

EvidenceThe most attractive supporting claim available for this chapter was examined and dropped. That is the method from the earlier chapters, applied to the author's own idea.

The duality

When Maya plays a real match, that is her performance. It is also practice. She has come to think the difference is not in the activity but in the mindset. As long as you can learn, reflect and improve from it, performance is practice. So the future she imagines is a duality. Fifteen minutes before a call, a seller switches into simulation mode and rehearses against everything the system knows about this account, this buyer and this deal stage. Then the live call, which becomes practice the moment feedback and reflection attach to it. The hours accumulate across both.

Learning point

The two halves solve different problems. Simulation removes the cost of failure, which is what currently stops sellers practising on anything that matters. Reflection on live calls removes the artificiality, which is what currently limits roleplay.

TheorySchön distinguishes reflection-in-action from reflection-on-action, and argues expertise develops mainly through the first. That is the harder one to support, and what a real-time coaching layer would be attempting.

Knowledge check 3 of 3
Someone proposes a simulation tool and says the business case is improved win rates. What is missing?

The problem is not that the claim is wrong. It is that nobody could check it.

Where Maya leaves it

Maya ends where she started, with the same question on the board. What has to be true for a seller to hold a conversation that moves revenue. Three programmes on, the answer is still environment, tooling, access, relationships, a named human in the loop, and content, in roughly that order. What has changed is how close enablement can get to the work itself. The simulator is not built. The argument for it is not evidence. The standard it has to meet is the one in every chapter before it.

Learning point

Close on the method, not the technology. The method survives whatever tooling arrives next.
